# Definitions and sector boundaries

Status: foundational vocabulary; proposed extensions remain conjectures.

## Spacetime curvature

**Spacetime curvature** is intrinsic curvature of a Lorentzian manifold, represented by the Riemann tensor and its contractions. It describes connection holonomy and geodesic deviation. It is not identified with curvature of one worldline.

## Geodesic curvature

**Geodesic curvature** measures departure of a parametrized curve from geodesic transport with respect to a declared connection and norm. A geodesic has zero covariant acceleration for an affine parameter. This is not identified with nonzero spacetime curvature: curved spacetime admits geodesics.

## Proper acceleration and timelike curvature

For a timelike worldline parametrized by proper time,

\[
u^\mu=\frac{dx^\mu}{d\tau},\qquad a^\mu=u^\nu\nabla_\nu u^\mu,
\qquad \kappa=\frac{\sqrt{a^\mu a_\mu}}{c^2}.
\]

**Proper acceleration** is measured by an ideal accelerometer. Under stated signature conventions, this candidate timelike curvature is nonnegative and vanishes for geodesic motion. A lower nonzero bound therefore needs new dynamics and is a conjecture, not a consequence of general relativity.

## Timelike curve

A **timelike curve** has timelike tangent and admits proper time. Definitions using proper time apply only to this sector unless independently generalized.

## Null curve

A **null curve** has null tangent and no proper-time parametrization. Null curvature requires an affine parameter, null frame, and invariants whose behavior under affine rescaling is controlled. Reusing the timelike formula by substitution is invalid.

## Field observable

A **field observable** is a gauge-invariant, preferably local or relational quantity constructed from fields and background/dynamical geometry. “Field curvature bounded by κ₀” has no accepted meaning here until such an observable and its dimensions are specified.

## Vacuum observable

A **vacuum observable** concerns a geometric vacuum solution or a quantum state and must be operationally defined. Vacuum is not an ordinary trajectory. Associating κ₀ with vacuum is a sectoral conjecture requiring a state-independent or explicitly state-dependent observable.

## Universal scale

UMCH hypothesizes one dimensional scale `κ₀`, but universality means only that the same parameter may occur in separately valid sectoral laws. It does not make spacetime curvature, geodesic curvature, proper acceleration, field observable, and vacuum observable identical.
