# Required limiting cases

## Standard limit: κ₀ → 0

Any proposed deformation must specify how `κ₀ → 0` recovers its claimed baseline:

- **special relativity:** inertial timelike worldlines and Poincaré covariance;
- **general relativity:** geodesic free fall, local Lorentz invariance, and Einstein dynamics unless explicitly modified;
- **classical electrodynamics:** declared Lorentz-force and radiation-reaction prescription;
- **quantum field theory:** declared free/interacting theory with unitarity and locality properties;
- **cosmology:** declared GR background and perturbation equations.

A singular or discontinuous limit is not automatically invalid, but must be derived and confronted with observations.

## Geodesic case

For `a^μ=0`, candidate timelike `κ=0`. A universal positive bound excludes ideal geodesic motion unless measured motion, effective dynamics, or the observable is redefined consistently. This is a central tension.

## Flat-spacetime case

Vanishing Riemann curvature does not imply that every trajectory has zero proper acceleration. Conversely, accelerated curves in Minkowski spacetime do not prove intrinsic spacetime curvature.

## Null case

Massless limit of timelike proper-time formulas can be singular. Null-sector definitions must be tested under affine rescaling.

## Weak-field and low-energy cases

Proposed effects must approach precision-tested Newtonian, post-Newtonian, atomic, and accelerator regimes within experimental uncertainties.
